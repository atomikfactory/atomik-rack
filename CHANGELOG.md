# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and this project aims to follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). Until 1.0, any release may break APIs and the patch and project formats.

## [Unreleased]

### Fixed
- **Piano roll tools.** Only the pencil creates notes now: with Select a press on the empty grid starts a rubber band (a click clears the selection, Shift or Ctrl adds to it) instead of drawing one. A note can be resized from its left edge as well as its right one, and with several notes selected every one of them changes by the same delta (snapped, with a minimum length), in one undo step like a group move. The mouse cursor shows what a press will do: a horizontal resize cursor over a note edge and during the drag, a move cursor over a note body, a crosshair for the pencil.
- **Sandboxed plugin editors on Wayland.** The plugin process now uses the X11 backend (XWayland) whenever `DISPLAY` is set, so a sandboxed CLAP or VST3 editor opens as an X11 window it can embed into instead of failing with "Wayland sessions cannot embed a plugin editor". The rack window stays native Wayland. In-process editors and sessions without `DISPLAY` still fall back to the parameter pane.

### Added
- **Spectrum and metering.** Two new effects that pass the audio through untouched (bit for bit, no latency, the bounce is identical with or without them) and show what they see; both are in the device browser with the effects and have the usual bypass. **Spectrum** (`atomik.spectrum`) draws an FFT of 1024 to 8192 points (Hann or Blackman-Harris) of the left, right, mid, side or sum, on a log axis from 20 Hz to 20 kHz over a dB range you choose, averaged over a time you choose, with peak hold and decay, an optional tilt of 3 or 4.5 dB per octave, a loudest-bin readout, or the 31 one-third-octave bands instead of the fine trace. **Phase Meter** (`atomik.phase-meter`) shows a goniometer (mid/side or plain left/right) from the recent samples, the stereo correlation from -1 to +1 over an averaging time, the balance and a width figure, peak, RMS and hold bars for each channel, the sample peak and the true peak, and the momentary, short-term and integrated loudness in LUFS (BS.1770-4, the meter of the mastering device) with a reset. The audio thread only copies each batch into a lock-free ring (`ScopeTap`, a new opt-in of the device SDK registered by the engine); the FFT, the averaging and the drawing run on the UI side, so the audio path costs 128 atomic stores a batch. The headless QA server gets `session_meters`, which renders a project offline up to a position and returns the readings (and fills the panels for `session_texts` and screenshots), so a generated -23 dBFS 1 kHz tone can be checked for -23 LUFS, correlation +1 and a 1 kHz line. Nothing new is saved in a project: the devices are ordinary devices, the project format is unchanged and older projects bounce exactly as before.
- **Audio to notes.** **Audio > Audio to Notes...** extracts the notes of the selected audio clip into a note clip that starts and ends with it, in one undo step. **Pitched** follows one voice or instrument (a YIN pitch tracker with a voicing decision, written in house) and cuts it into notes by pitch stability and by re-attacks, so vibrato and a glide inside the pitch drift stay one note; the key is the pitch rounded to a semitone, the length follows the voicing and the decay, the velocity the level. **Drums** finds the hits of a loop and sorts them into kick, snare, closed and open hat (and a kick with a hat on top) from the spectrum after each onset, on the General MIDI keys 36, 38, 42 and 46. The panel sets the sensitivity, the shortest note, the range, the pitch drift, open hats, **Beats timing** (starts rounded to 1/4 to 1/32, or free) and the target: a new poly synth track or the note track selected in the rack. The notes follow the tempo map under the clip and the warp time map of a warped clip (a transposed clip adds its transposition; a clip in Repitch mode is refused), a reversed clip is read as it plays, and streamed assets are read in chunks. The analysis runs on a worker thread (at most ten minutes of a clip) while the song plays. `session_audio_notes` runs it in a headless MCP session. No file format change.
- **Freeze and bounce in place.** **Track > Freeze Track** renders the selected tracks through their instrument and every effect of their channel's insert chain and plays the render instead, so none of those devices run (the point: CPU). The track's clips, instrument and effects stay untouched; **Unfreeze Track** brings them back exactly (one undo step), **Unfreeze Track and Keep Audio** also leaves the render as a clip on a new audio track. **Freeze Track up to Selected Device** stops at the instrument or the insert effect selected in the rack, and the effects after it stay live; the channel's fader, pan, sends and output are never frozen. **Freeze with Tail** (on by default) keeps the decay of reverbs and delays past the end of the clips, cut where it falls below -90 dBFS. The render runs through the offline bounce on a worker thread (progress in the status line, **Cancel Freeze**, several tracks queue) and is stored in the project's audio folder; a render of a track that changed meanwhile is thrown away. The song keeps its sound: the project bounced with a track frozen equals the unfrozen bounce bit for bit when the chain has no tail or latency, and a chain with latency stays aligned with the other tracks. A frozen track shows a snowflake in its header and its render in ice blue in place of its clips; editing it, its devices or the tempo is refused with a message that says to unfreeze. **Bounce in Place** (and **and Mute Originals**) renders the selected clips through the track's chain into one clip on a new audio track and freezes nothing. Plugins render through the plugin sandbox. Project format 25 saves a frozen track (version 24 files load unchanged). The QA server runs the commands (`session_command` waits for the render) and lists `frozen` in the inspection.
- **Time-stretch and pitch-shift (warp).** Audio clips can follow the song tempo and be transposed without changing their length. **Warp** (Audio > Warp Audio, or Warp Settings...) plays a clip through a time map in beats at the tempo it was recorded at, so the audio stays on the grid when the song tempo changes or ramps; three engines are written in house, with no new dependency: **Beats** keeps every transient of drums and percussive loops where the tempo puts it and its attack intact and stretches the audio between hits, **Tonal** is a phase vocoder with phase locking for sustained and polyphonic material, and **Repitch** resamples (speed and pitch together, the cheap one). **Transpose** (Audio > Transpose, up or down a semitone or an octave, or the panel with semitones and cents, up to two octaves either way) changes pitch without changing length in Beats and Tonal. The source tempo comes from **Warp Loop as N Bars** (the clip becomes N bars long at the tempo its length implies), **Warp to Song Tempo** (an estimate from the transients of the audio, snapped to the exact tempo of the clip when it holds whole beats) or the panel's source tempo, DETECT TEMPO, /2 and x2. **Quantize Audio** (Edit menu and clip context menu) finds the transients of the selected clips and moves them to the grid of the quantize options by its strength with warp markers, stretching locally between them, with the ends of the clip pinned; markers survive trims and splits. The clip shows a `W` (with the transposition, `W +3`) and its markers as thin lines, and its waveform follows the stretch. A warped clip is rendered off the audio thread into a cached buffer (at most two background workers, 512 MiB, replaced when a setting, the tempo or the clip changes) and plays unwarped until it is ready; a bounce renders every buffer first, so it is never dry and stays bit for bit repeatable. Reverse and warp exclude each other; Strip Silence needs the clip unwarped. Project format 24; every earlier project plays and bounces as before. The QA server's inspection lists a clip's warp.
- **Audio editing.** Audio clips get fades and crossfades, clip gain and gain envelopes, reverse, normalise and strip silence, all non-destructive. A clip has a fade in and out whose shape is Linear, Equal power, Exponential or S-curve (Audio > Fade Shape, or the clip context menu), the fade and gain handles on the clip as before. A clip that starts over the end of an earlier one on the same track now **crossfades** with it: over the overlap the earlier clip fades out and the later one fades in, equal power by default (Audio > Crossfade picks Linear, Exponential, S-curve or Off, a hard cut), and the zone is drawn on the later clip; a clip inside another still hides it. **Gain envelopes** are drawn on the clip over the waveform: click with the pencil to add a point and drag it, drag points with the select tool, right-click or use the eraser to remove one, Audio > Clear Gain Envelope; the envelope ramps smoothly between points, sits in clip time and follows the audio when the left edge is trimmed. Clip gain also has Audio > Clip Gain (up and down 1 dB, reset) next to the gain handle, and Audio > Fades sets a 5 ms declick or no fades. **Reverse** (Edit > Reverse Audio) plays the clip's span backwards and works with trims, fades and envelopes (an `R` marks the clip). **Normalise** (Edit > Normalise Audio) sets the clip gain so the peak of what each selected clip plays reaches 0 dBFS. **Strip Silence** (Edit > Strip Silence...) opens a panel (threshold, minimum silence, pad before and after, fade) and cuts the selected clips into their sounds, one undo step. Project format 23; files from before load with every clip's crossfade off, so old overlaps stay hard cuts and old bounces keep their bytes. The QA server's inspection lists audio clips with their gain, fades, reverse, envelope and crossfade.
- **Track organisation.** Tracks can be put in folders (Track > Group Tracks, Ctrl+Shift+G, on the selected tracks or the tracks of the selected clips; shift-click or ctrl-click on headers selects several). A folder is a row above its first track with a chevron that collapses and expands it, a name typed in place (double-click, or Track > Rename Folder), M, S and R buttons that mute, solo or arm every track of the folder in one undo step, and a summary of the clips of its tracks; folders nest up to 8 deep, Track > Ungroup (or Delete on a selected folder) dissolves one and keeps its tracks, and Move Track Up and Down (Alt+Up and Alt+Down) move a track one place at a time, in and out of folders, or a folder with everything in it. A folder does not touch the audio or the routing. A track can have a colour (header and clips; Colour submenu of the track header menu) and a comment (Track > Track Comment, typed in the header; a note mark shows it and its first line is a tooltip). An automation lane folds to a thin strip with the chevron at the left of its header (Track > Fold Automation Lanes folds them all), and the take rows that are open stay open with the project. Track > Track Height sets the selected tracks, or all, to Small, Medium, Large or Fit (as tall as the view lets them be). Project format 22 saves it all (a project without any of it differs from its version 21 file only in the version line; version 21 files open unchanged); the MCP inspection lists folders, colours and comments, and the Track menu commands are session commands.
- **MIDI file import and export.** Standard MIDI Files (type 0 and 1) in and out of the app. File > Export MIDI Clip, Track and Song write the selected note clip or track (type 0) or every note track (type 1: a conductor track with the song name, tempo map, time signatures and markers, then one track per note track, named after its device) with pitch bend, aftertouch and CC lanes as controller events; they write what plays (loops, offsets and swing applied, muted notes and clips left out) at 15 360 ticks per quarter note, the app's own resolution, so a round trip keeps every tick. File > Import MIDI puts a file in one clip on the selected note track at the playhead, or makes one new track per MIDI track and channel when no note track is selected; File > Import MIDI as Song makes new tracks from the start and also takes the file's tempo map, time signatures and markers. A `.mid` file dropped on the window makes new tracks at the playhead. Every import is one undo step. The reader handles running status, SysEx and unknown meta events (skipped), a note-on with velocity 0 as a note-off, hanging notes, type 0 files split by channel and other divisions, and refuses type 2 files, SMPTE time and corrupt files with a message instead of changing anything. Audio and automation lanes, range markers and program changes are not exported; a tempo ramp is exported as a staircase. No project format change. The QA server gains `session_midi`.
- **Quantize, swing, grooves and humanize.** Edit > Quantize Options (also in the clip and note context menus) opens a panel for the selected clips of the arrangement, or the selected notes of the piano roll (every note of the open clip when none is selected): a grid from 1/4 to 1 bar with triplets, a strength that moves notes part of the way, swing from 50 % to 75 %, a groove, humanize timing and velocity with a seed, and an option to snap the note ends too. A groove is a named template of per-step timing offsets and velocity scales: six come built in (Straight, Swing 55, 60 and 66 %, a shuffled 1/8 and an accented 1/16) and EXTRACT GROOVE FROM CLIP measures one from a clip's own feel and keeps it in the song, shared by every track, until DELETE. The grid is the song's, so a clip that starts off the grid still lands on the bar lines. Humanize is the same on every run (it comes from the seed, the clip and each note's own position), so a bounce is reproducible; another seed gives another take. Applying is baked into the note times, one undo step over all selected clips, and playback and bounces are unchanged; two notes of one key that land on the same tick both stay (the later moves one tick on). Audio clips, controller lane points and the takes of a take group are left alone. Ctrl+K (Cmd+K on macOS, as in Reason) quantizes the selected notes of the piano roll or the selected clips of the arrangement at once with the panel's last settings (Q does the same in the legacy profile), Edit > Quantize does it from the menu (enabled with a selection, like Cut and Copy), and the piano roll's toolbar has a QUANT button that does it too, with a `...` button (or a right click on QUANT) that opens the panel. Project format 21 (older files load without grooves); `project_inspect` lists the song's grooves.
- **Loop recording and takes.** Recording over an enabled loop now stacks every pass as a take of one take group on the track (note and audio), instead of laying overlapping clips: the last pass plays and the others are kept. In the arrangement a thin comp strip under the lane shows which take plays where (each take has its own colour) and its chevron opens one row per take. Click the name of a take or its row to play that take alone, drag across a take row to comp a range from it (snapped, Escape cancels), use the cross to delete a take and FLATTEN to keep what plays as plain clips and drop the takes. Every action, and a whole recording, is one undo step. The comp is compiled into ordinary clips, so playback and bounces are unchanged, and a cut inside an audio take gets a 5 ms fade at the splice so it does not click. Punch and non-loop recording are unchanged. Project format 20 (older files load without groups); `project_inspect` lists the groups.
- **MIDI controller data.** Pitch bend, aftertouch (channel and per key) and CC lanes in note clips, played to devices and plugins, recorded from live MIDI input and edited in the piano roll. A CONTROLLERS field in the scale bar opens an area under the velocity lane with chips for Bend, Press, Mod 1, Vol 7, Pan 10, Expr 11 and Sust 64, a stepper for any other CC and one for poly aftertouch; draw with the pencil or the select tool (one point per grid step), drag points, delete them with the eraser or a right click, Ctrl or Cmd click toggles ramp and step, and a whole stroke is one undo step. Recording keeps every pitch bend, aftertouch and CC that reaches an armed track, per loop pass, next to the notes. Playback sends the events sample-accurately, restores each controller's value when you play or locate in the middle of a clip, and zeroes pitch bend, aftertouch and the sustain pedal on stop. The poly synth now follows pitch bend (two semitones) and the mod wheel (vibrato). Project format 19 (older files load without lanes).
- **Automation recording.** Arm an automation lane with the W button on its header, pick how it writes with the OVR / TCH / LTC button next to it (Overwrite replaces the whole pass, Touch writes only while you hold the control, Latch writes from your first touch until you stop), arm a track or just the lane, and press Record: moves of the lane's parameter (a knob drag, a typed value, a plugin's own window, a container macro) become automation points. The recorded points are thinned (Ramer-Douglas-Peucker, 0.5 % of the parameter's range) so a drag does not leave hundreds of points. While a lane is being written the old automation does not fight the control; a loop recording writes one clip per pass, and a whole take, whatever the number of lanes and passes, is **one undo step**. The write mode is saved in the project (project format 18; older files load on Touch), the arm state is not. MIDI controller input is not recorded yet.
- **Note chase.** A note that spans the play position now sounds when you press Play, locate while playing, or the loop wraps, instead of staying silent until its next note. Each track has a **Chase Notes** item (checked by default) in its header's context menu; switch it off for a track that should play only from its note starts. Live edits never retrigger a note that is already sounding. The setting is one undo step and is saved with the project (format 17: a track that does not chase has `"no_chase": true`; versions 1 to 16 load with chase on). The QA server's project and session status report `chase` for each track.
- **Relink missing audio.** File > Relink Missing Audio... (enabled while a project's audio files are missing) lists them with a LOCATE... button each and SEARCH FOLDER.... LOCATE... takes any audio file: it is imported into the project bundle and every clip and sampler, slicer and drum pad zone that used the missing asset is switched to it in one undo step (the old, missing state comes back with undo); a file with the very same audio only restores the bundle's file, with no document change. SEARCH FOLDER... scans a folder (8 levels, 50 000 entries at most, off the UI thread) and relinks the missing files it finds by name, in any case, leaving the rest listed; a toast says how many were relinked and how many are still missing. A file that does not decode, or that is too short for the zones that used the old one, is reported by name and changes nothing. The QA server gains `session_relink`, and `session_bounce` now renders from the session's bundle, so audio projects bounce in a session.
- **QA server (`atomik-mcp`).** A development tool, not part of the release archives: a Model Context Protocol server over stdio that lets an MCP client bounce projects and patches, measure and compare WAV files (levels, silence, loudness windows, spectrum, bit-exact or within a tolerance), and drive a headless app (menu commands, pointer, keys, the texts the UI draws, PNG screenshots, undo, save, and a bounce of the open document), with every path confined to the working directory and `--root` folders. [docs/MCP.md](docs/MCP.md) has the tools and the QA recipes that [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) now asks for. `atomik-ui` gains an optional `cpu-canvas` feature (the `vello_cpu` canvas its preview tests used, now library code).
- **Scales and chords in the piano roll.** A clip has a scale (a root and one of fifteen: Major, the minors and the modes, the pentatonics, Blues, Whole Tone, Phrygian Dominant and Hungarian Minor; no scale is Chromatic), chosen from a new scale bar under the piano roll's toolbar or the notes context menu's Scale submenu, or found by **Detect** from the notes. In-scale rows are tinted, out-of-scale rows are dark, the root rows are marked and the keyboard names them. The scale is saved with the clip (project format 16; older files load without one). **Snap to Scale** (`Shift+S`) puts pencil-drawn notes and vertical drags on the nearest in-scale key (a tie goes down) and makes Transpose Up and Down move by scale degree, and **Conform Selection to Scale** moves the selected notes onto the scale in one step. **Fold** (`Shift+F`, or the Fold field) hides the keys outside the scale, or every key the clip does not use, and drawing, dragging, band select, the keyboard and the key zoom all work on the folded rows. The **chord stamp** (twenty-one chords, four inversions, and diatonic triads, sevenths and ninths built from the scale) makes the pencil drop a whole chord that you can drag to length and that sounds when you click; **Chordify** (`Shift+C`) turns each selected note into the chord, **Arpeggiate** turns each chord of the selection into an arpeggio over its length (up, down, up-down, down-up, random or as played, at the current snap, triplets included, with a gate and one to three octaves), and **Strum** delays the notes of each chord one after the other and keeps their ends. Every edit is one undo step. The context menus gain submenus for all of it, and the snap to scale and fold choices are kept between sessions.
- **Deep zoom and finer editing.** The time zoom goes down to the sample: a tick is up to 16 px wide (about 10 px per sample at 120 BPM and 48 kHz), the ruler grid follows with lines at 1/32, 1/64, 1/128 notes and down to single ticks, and below the bars the ruler names its lines `bar.beat.sixteenth.tick`. Snap gains 1/32, 1/64, the triplets 1/4T, 1/8T, 1/16T and 1/32T and **Grid**, which snaps to the lines the ruler shows at the current zoom (the selector in the arrangement and the piano roll cycles through them; `sequencer.set_snap` takes `thirtysecond`, `sixtyfourth`, `quarter_triplet`, `eighth_triplet`, `sixteenth_triplet`, `thirtysecond_triplet` and `grid`, and the piano roll's Quantize uses the same step). Waveforms in the arrangement, the sample editor and the slice editor read the samples themselves when you zoom in below 256 frames per pixel, and below one frame per pixel draw a line through the sample values with a dot on every sample (audio streamed from disk keeps the finest peak level); the editors' wave zoom reaches 32 px per frame whatever the length of the file. Vertical zoom: drag the bottom edge of a track header to set that track's height (24 to 400 px, a double-click resets it), Alt+wheel in the arrangement zooms every track (Shift+H and Shift+G, Shift+Z resets the zoom and the heights), headers shorter than 52 px keep one line, and the piano roll zooms its keys the same way (Alt+wheel, 6 to 48 px, Shift+H, Shift+G and Shift+Z; the white keys are named from 16 px). Track heights are saved in the project (format 15, versions 1 to 14 load unchanged: a song may carry `"track_heights"`, written only when non-empty); the vertical zooms are saved with the window state.
- **Markers and range markers.** The arrangement has a marker strip under its ruler (View, Marker Strip; on by default and kept between sessions) with named point markers and named ranges (Intro, Verse...). Add a marker at the playhead with Shift+M (Transport, Add Marker), by a double-click or the pencil on the strip, or from the marker list; drag it to move it (snapped by the current snap, the disable-snap keys free it), double-click its label to type a new name in place, and delete it with the eraser, Delete or the context menu, which also has a small colour palette. Click a marker to locate the playhead there; `,` and `.` (and the Transport menu) go to the previous and next marker. Ranges are bands in a row above the markers: Transport, Add Range Marker from Loop (or the strip's context menu, from the loop or the selected clips) creates one, a drag of the body moves it, the edges resize it, and a click sets the loop to its range (Loop This Range and Select Clips in Range are in its context menu). View, Marker List docks a list of every marker and range at the right edge of the arrangement with its position as bar.beat.tick through the meter map (and the end of a range); a click on a row locates (a range row also sets the loop), a double-click on a name renames it, `x` deletes it, and `+ Marker` and `+ Range` add. Every edit is one undo step and a drag is one step. Markers follow the bars when the tempo or the meter changes. Project format 14 (versions 1 to 13 load unchanged): a song may carry `"markers"` and `"ranges"`, written only when there are some. The legacy key profile is unchanged (the commands are in the menus).
- **Tempo and meter changes.** The song can change tempo with steps and linear ramps (the tempo moves in BPM over ticks) and change time signature on any bar line. A tempo lane in the arrangement (View, Tempo Lane; off by default and kept between sessions) plots the tempo curve: add a point with the pencil or a double-click, drag it (snapped in time, whole BPMs up and down, Shift for tenths, Alt for no snap), delete it with the eraser or Delete, and right-click for Ramp to Next, Set Tempo... and Delete. Right-click the ruler to insert a meter change (2/4 to 12/8) at that bar; its tag sits next to the bar number and can be dragged to another bar, changed or deleted. The ruler, the grid, snapping, the bar keys, new clips, recording and the metronome follow the meter map, and notes, audio clips and loop points land on the exact frame of the ramp live and in the bounce (one tempo map in `atomik-core` replaces the four copies of the conversion). Tempo-synced devices and Mod Source follow the tempo and the meter at every 64-frame batch. The transport shows the tempo and the meter at the playhead, and typing a tempo changes the event in effect there. Project format 13 (versions 1 to 12 load unchanged): a tempo event may carry `"ramp": true`, and `"meter"` is a list of `{"bar", "num", "den"}` changes. The metronome now clicks on the beats of the meter, an eighth in 6/8 rather than a quarter.
- **Plugin sandbox.** Every CLAP and VST3 plugin instance runs in its own `atomik-plugin-host` process, so a plugin that crashes or hangs no longer takes the application down. A crashed plugin's device shows CRASHED with the reason (dry for an effect, silent otherwise) while audio goes on, and RELOAD starts it again from its last saved state. Audio is exchanged through shared memory in the same 64-frame blocks, with no added latency; live, a block the plugin answers late passes dry. Bounces wait for every block, so a sandboxed bounce is byte-identical to an in-process one. Editors open in a window of the plugin's process. Settings > Plugins has "Run plugins in a separate process (recommended)", on by default (applies after restarting); `atomik-render` has `--plugin-sandbox on|off`. Release archives and the macOS bundle include the new `atomik-plugin-host` program.
- **Output buses.** The console can hold named output buses (`atomik.bus` strips with the channel's controls): channels and buses route to the master, to another bus (loops are refused) or to a free Audio I/O stereo pair, with commands to create, rename, reorder, delete and route to a new bus, each one undo step, and a solo that keeps a soloed bus's feeders and a soloed strip's buses audible. Project format 12 saves the bus list (omitted when there are none; versions 1 to 11 load unchanged). In the console, bus strips sit between the channels and the master with the full channel strip, an output selector on every strip (Master, buses, Audio I/O pairs, New bus...; pairs in use or beyond the project's outputs are greyed with the reason), rename by double-click, a bus context menu (Rename, Delete bus), drag to reorder and meters. Route to New Output Bus (Ctrl/Cmd+G, Mixer menu) puts the selected channels on a new bus. In the rack the plate reads `Output: Bus <name>` or `Output: Audio I/O 3+4`; an Audio I/O pair no longer shows the warning colour.
- **Mod Source.** A new modulation device with two LFOs (seven shapes with morph, free or tempo-synced rate, key sync, rate and amount CV inputs) and an ADSR envelope triggered by notes or a gate CV, with CV outputs for the rest of the rack. Its panel has a shape display for each LFO and a live scrolling scope of both LFOs and the envelope, and the device browser lists it in a new Modulators group.
- **Multi-column rack.** The rack can have several columns side by side (saved in the project, project format 11; older files load as one column). Add Column and Remove Column are in the rack context menu; an empty column stays until it is removed, and removing a column moves its devices to the one on its left. Each column scrolls with the rack, which now also scrolls sideways to reach all of them.
- **Dragging between columns.** A device, or a selected group, can be dragged to any place in any column; the drop line shows where it lands, and dropping to the right of the last column makes a new column. The whole move is one undo step. The rack scrolls by itself when you drag near its edges.
- **Drag devices from the browser.** Drag a row of the device browser onto the rack to create the device exactly where you drop it, in any column; an effect is routed as if the device above the drop point were selected. Release over the browser, or press Esc, to cancel. A double-click still adds below the selection.
- **Rack navigator.** A strip at the right edge of the rack (View, Rack Navigator; on by default) shows every column as small coloured blocks and the part of the rack you are looking at. Click to centre the view there, drag to scroll, use the mouse wheel as over the rack; inside a container it shows the container's column. The choice is kept between sessions.
- **Paste and plugin drops in columns.** Paste from the rack's context menu lands where you right-clicked, and a keyboard paste lands below the selected device in its column. A plugin that is neither an instrument nor an effect can be dragged from the browser into a column or the new-column zone like any other device, and the new-column zone now stays on screen while you drag, whatever the window width.
- **CV inputs on the effects, console channels and instruments.** One convention: a CV socket `<target>_cv_in` with a bipolar `<target>_cv_trim` (−1..+1, default +1). The stereo delay (mix, feedback), reverb, modulation and distortion (mix, drive), the EQ (gain of the four shelf and peak bands) and every console channel (fader, pan, the eight sends) take one; so do the poly synth (pitch, resonance, level, pan), the sampler (resonance, level, pan), the drum machine (global level and pitch, see below) and the loop slicer (level, and cutoff through a new global low-pass, `filter.cutoff`, which is open by default). Unplugged, nothing changes, and older patches and projects load as before.
- **Gates and global CV on the drum machine.** Two global CV inputs, `level_cv_in` (±24 dB) and `pitch_cv_in` (±24 semitones, also bending voices that are already sounding, glided over each 64-sample batch), each with a bipolar trim. Per pad, a gate in (`padN_gate_cv_in`: a rise above 0.5 plays the pad, velocity from the level, the fall releases a gate-mode pad) and a gate out (`padN_gate_cv_out`: high from every hit, from notes, the sequencer or the gate in, until its note-off). The back panel shows the 16 gate pairs and the two CV jacks with their trims.
- **CV activity indicators.** On the back of the rack, a CV cable that carries signal glows and lights both of its jacks, brighter for a stronger signal and fading smoothly when it falls silent.
- **Nudge by beat or tick.** In the piano roll, the selected notes can move by one beat or by one tick, whatever the snap: `Ctrl+Shift+Left/Right` (beat) and `Ctrl+Alt+Left/Right` (tick) in the standard profile, `Shift+Left/Right` and `Alt+Left/Right` in the legacy one (`Cmd` for `Ctrl` on macOS).
- **Console keys are commands.** The console's arrow, Page Up/Down, Home and End keys are now `mixer.*` commands in the Mixer context (the same keys as before, Shift for fine steps), so they show in the cheat sheet and can be rebound in Settings, Keyboard. The console also owns the keyboard as a text field while a channel name is being typed, so Space, Delete and the letters never run commands then; Enter commits and Esc cancels.
- **Editor keys follow the keymap.** Delete and Backspace in the sample editor (remove the selected zone) and the slice editor (merge the selected slice into the previous one) are now the `edit.delete` command, so they follow Settings, Keyboard and the active profile. The sample, slice, step, sequencer and container editors no longer read Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y themselves: undo and redo are `edit.undo` and `edit.redo` everywhere, and rebinding them takes effect in the editors. Esc and typing a name are unchanged.
- **Time signature in the transport bar.** Double-click the meter readout and type a signature (`3/4`, or a bare `3` to keep the beat unit, 1 to 32 over 2, 4, 8 or 16): it changes the meter change in effect at the playhead, undoable like the tempo. Before, the first signature could not be changed at all (a 4/4 start has no ruler tag).
- **Ctrl+Shift+wheel zooms vertically.** In the arrangement (track heights) and the piano roll (key heights), next to Alt+wheel; Ctrl+wheel stays the time zoom.
- **Multi-select and rubber band in the arrangement.** Shift+click adds a clip to the selection, Ctrl+click toggles it, across tracks (Ctrl+click is also how a copy drag starts, see below). A rubber band selects the clips it touches: drag from empty space below the last track, or Shift or Ctrl+drag on an empty lane (a plain drag on a lane still draws a clip); Shift or Ctrl add to the selection, a plain band replaces it, and Esc cancels it. Delete, copy, paste, duplicate, split, mute and the drag act on every selected clip, as before, and every selected clip is outlined.
- **Drag clips onto other lanes.** Dragging clips vertically onto another track moves them there (the time follows the horizontal drag), and Ctrl+drag drops copies instead (the clips stay put and the copies are selected). Each clip lands on the lane of its own kind: a note clip on the note lane, an audio clip on an audio track's lane, an automation clip on the lane of the same parameter; a target track without that lane (a note clip onto an audio track) ignores the drop and changes nothing. A group keeps its relative tracks and offsets, and the whole gesture, including the horizontal moves, is one undo step (a drag of several clips used to leave one step per clip and per move). A ghost shows where the drop lands. New `WorkflowCommand::MoveClips`; no project format change, since a clip belongs to its lane in the file.

### Fixed
- **Track header click acts like a console strip.** Clicking a track header in the arrangement (or stepping with Up and Down) now selects its strip in the console, auto-arms the track as a channel selection does (disarming the tracks an earlier auto-arm armed), selects the device in the rack so the live keyboard plays it, and a plain click hands the keys to the rack.

- **Device search.** Typing in the device browser's search kept the scroll of the full list, so the few matches (Serum for "ser") sat above the visible panel; the list now returns to the top on every edit.

### Removed
- **The plugin warning.** Turning plugins on in Settings > Plugins, or LOAD on a project that uses plugins, no longer shows a warning to confirm: plugins now run in their own processes by default.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-21

First public version. Windows (x86-64) is the primary platform and is tested by hand; the test suite runs in CI on Windows, macOS and Linux, Linux has been tried on Ubuntu 26.04, and macOS has not been run on real hardware yet. Project files are format version 10 (versions 1 to 9 load).

### Added
- **Engine.** A real-time engine with fixed 64-frame batches that is allocation-free and lock-free on the render path, hot edits of the rack while audio plays, multicore rendering with bit-identical output for any worker count and host block size, plugin delay compensation across the whole graph, and cable validation (types, direction, cycles).
- **Rack and console.** A device rack with audio, CV and note cables and front and back views; a console with a channel strip per instrument (gain, filters, dynamics, EQ, insert chain, 8 sends, pan, fader), solo and mute, meters; a locked master section with 8 send buses and FX returns, a bus compressor with a key input, a control-room output and the Audio I/O device.
- **Devices.** Poly synth, line mixer, stereo delay, sampler, drum machine, loop slicer, audio track, reverb, compressor, 6-band EQ, distortion with oversampling, modulation (chorus, flanger, phaser), mastering suite, the Character effect (16 presets, Transform macro), step sequencer, arpeggiator and the container device with macros and note splits.
- **Plugin hosting.** CLAP and VST3 instruments and effects as rack devices, with an out-of-process scanner, a plugin cache, editor windows, state saving, crash journal and recovery snapshots. VST2 is not supported.
- **Arrangement and audio.** Note, automation and audio clips, a piano roll, sample, step, slice and container editors, import of WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP3 and OGG, audio recording with latency compensation, disk streaming for large files, and project bundle folders with content-addressed audio.
- **Workflow.** A data-driven, remappable keymap with a standard and a legacy profile, tools (select, pencil, eraser, razor), a clipboard for clips, notes, points, devices and tracks, context menus, rack multi-selection and an auto-routing creation chain.
- **Tools.** `atomik` (the application), `atomik-render` (offline bounce of patches and projects to WAV), `atomik-play` (a live patch player) and `atomik-plugin-scan` (the plugin scanner).
- **Audio and MIDI I/O.** Live audio through WASAPI, CoreAudio and ALSA (and ASIO as an opt-in build feature that needs the Steinberg SDK, which is not included), and MIDI input with exact event timing.
- **Project hygiene.** GPL-3.0-or-later licence, contribution guide with DCO sign-off and test-driven development rules, code of conduct, security policy, CI for Windows, macOS and Linux (fmt, clippy, tests, cargo-deny, coverage), architecture, device, testing and keyboard documentation, and example projects.
- Project format 10: per-note mute. A muted note is a fifth element `true` in the note's array; muted notes stay in the clip and are skipped by playback and bounce. Versions 1 to 9 still load. New `SetClipsMuted` workflow command mutes or unmutes several clips in one undo step.
- Mute in the editors: `M` mutes or unmutes the selected clips in the arrangement (any clip kind, one undo step) and the selected notes in the piano roll (in the step editor, the cell under the pointer); Mute / Unmute items in the clip and notes context menus; an `M` button in each clip header; muted notes and step cells are painted hollow.
- **File browser.** A docked panel (View, File Browser, `F4`) with a folder tree (Favourites, Tags, Bookmarks, drives) over a file list of folders and audio files, a search field (words, `#tag`, `fav`, optionally in sub-folders), instant preview on click with a 5 ms crossfade when switching (live only, outside the routing and the meters), favourites, tags and bookmarked folders kept in `library.json` in the configuration folder, and drag to the arrangement, sample editor and sampler panels like an OS file drop. New crate `atomik-library`; `atomik --file-browser [DIR]` shows the panel at start. See [docs/ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md#6j-the-file-browser).
- Release archives for Windows (zip), macOS (universal `Atomik Rack.app`, ad-hoc signed, in a zip) and Linux (tar.gz), each smoke-tested before a draft release is created, with per-archive `.sha256` files and a `SHA256SUMS` file.

### Known limitations
- Feedback loops are rejected; insert and return loops use stages instead.
- Parameter changes take effect at 64-frame boundaries (devices smooth audible parameters); notes are sample-accurate.
- No time-stretch for audio clips and no relinking of missing audio files.
- Plugins run inside the application process, so a plugin crash takes the application down.
- No note chase on play or locate.
- Linux VST3 editors that need file-descriptor handlers do not work, and there is no editor embedding on Wayland (the parameter pane is the fallback).
- Release builds are not code-signed or notarized (the macOS app is signed ad hoc); see the Install section of the README for the first start.
